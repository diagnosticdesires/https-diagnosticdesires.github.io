The Teal Threshold
I didn’t expect a stranger to alter the climate of my nervous system, but that is how this season began — not with conversation, not with intention, but with a sequence of oddly patterned proximities in a study space I inhabit almost ritualistically.
For days he was just a silhouette in my peripheral field: curly hair, quiet intensity, a kind of self‑contained confidence that didn’t seek an audience. Then one afternoon, at the door, our eyes met. Brief. Neutral. But something in me registered it as a shift — a threshold crossed.
After that, the geometry of the room changed. He began appearing in my field in ways that felt deliberate without being declarative. A teal shirt one day — unexpectedly crisp, unexpectedly striking — made him suddenly visible in a way that felt almost narrative. He hovered at the edges of conversations, never entering them. He sat near me, then far, then near again. I noticed him noticing me. I noticed myself noticing that.
None of this was romantic. It was diagnostic. A study of how desire behaves when it has no declared object, only a climate. How attention arranges itself. How a stranger becomes a variable in one’s internal weather system.
The climate lasted barely a few weeks. It felt like a season.
This blog is an atlas of those seasons — not confessions, not longing — but the mechanics of desire as it appears in the field: ambient, patterned, sometimes absurd, sometimes tender, always instructive.
